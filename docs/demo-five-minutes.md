# Five-minute presentation route — three people

Use fictional scenarios only. Rehearse on the exact laptop. Generate a comparison before presenting if solver delay would consume the pitch.

| Time | Presenter | Show and explain |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–1:10 | Person 1 | Team and student waiting-time problem. Overview: deliberately inefficient synthetic baseline, 15,000 gap hours/week; this is a demo, not measured institutional impact. |
| 1:10–3:10 | Person 2 | Recommendations → Compare three plans. At most five sections, five seconds per plan. Explain time saved vs fewest changes vs balanced burden. Show benefits and harms, not just the score. Expand classes; choose a plan and show normal review. |
| 3:10–4:20 | Person 3 | Workforce shortage evidence and human decision boundary. Explain imported schedule review and incomplete resource facts. |
| 4:20–5:00 | Person 1 | Local-first architecture, deterministic solver/validation, optional local AI, future pilot and closing. |

Example baseline run: 1,800 hours / 5 moves / 225 students helped; 300 hours / 1 move / 75 helped; 1,500 hours / 5 moves / 375 helped. Actual bounded results can vary; read the screen. Zero harm is measured for the shown run, not guaranteed.

If showing S001 moved to Sunday 10:00, say 77 total conflicts including 75 student overlaps, one professor overlap and one room overlap. Historical deck notes mentioning a fourth speaker or zero harm as a universal constraint are superseded by this route and release evidence. AI benchmark evidence is synthetic and historical; do not make it the unrehearsed live centerpiece.
